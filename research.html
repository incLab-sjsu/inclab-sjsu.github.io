Keeping upright and moving safely looks effortless, but it depends on a continuous conversation between the brain, the senses, and the muscles. In the Intelligent Neuromotor Control Lab we measure that conversation. We quantify how people stand and walk, how stable and coordinated those movements are, and what the nervous system is doing while it produces them. We then ask how the picture changes with age, with fatigue, and in neurodevelopmental conditions such as autism, where motor differences have long been under-studied in adulthood.

Our work combines laboratory measures of movement with data-driven analysis, with the long-term aim of building practical tools that detect changes in neuromotor function early and support independent, safe mobility.

How we work

Our studies bring participants into the lab for careful, standardized assessments of standing balance and walking, and record signals that reflect how the nervous system is controlling those tasks. We analyze not only how much people move, but how variable and how coordinated that movement is over time, because those patterns carry information about control that averages miss. Where it helps, we pair these measures with assessments of cognition and everyday function, so that findings connect to the outcomes that matter to people's lives.
