Keeping upright and moving safely looks effortless, but it depends on a continuous conversation between the brain, the senses, and the muscles. In the Intelligent Neuromotor Control Lab we measure that conversation. We quantify how people stand and walk, how stable and coordinated those movements are, and what the nervous system is doing while it produces them. We then ask how the picture changes with age, with fatigue, with neurodegeneration, and across neurodiverse populations (such as autistic adults and adults with Down syndrome), whose motor health in adulthood has long been under-studied.

Our work combines laboratory measures of movement with data-driven analysis, with the long-term aim of building practical tools that detect changes in neuromotor function early and support independent, safe mobility.

How we work

Our studies bring participants in for careful, standardized assessments of standing balance and walking. We analyze not only how much people move, but how variable and how coordinated that movement is over time, because those patterns carry information about control that averages miss. Where a study calls for it, we pair these measures with assessments of cognition, brain activity, and everyday function, so that findings connect to the outcomes that matter to people's lives. All of our studies with human participants are reviewed and approved by the San José State University Institutional Review Board before recruitment begins.
